01

Overview

This project compares three-class screening and five-class ICDRS severity classification from fundus images, with particular attention to preprocessing and class imbalance.

02

My role

  • Designed a preprocessing pipeline combining black-border removal and CLAHE contrast enhancement.
  • Implemented class-balancing strategies using random oversampling, on-the-fly augmentation, and class weighting.
  • Compared ResNet50 and DenseNet121 across two classification settings and analyzed sensitivity alongside overall accuracy.

03

Data

  • APTOS 2019 Blindness Detection: 3,662 fundus images.
  • Training / validation / test: approximately 70/10/20, corresponding to 2,562 / 367 / 733 images.
  • Original class counts for grades 0–4: 1,805 / 370 / 999 / 193 / 295.

04

Approach

  • Removed black borders, applied CLAHE, resized images to 224 × 224 pixels, and normalized inputs.
  • Used random oversampling, a class weight of 1.5 for severe cases, and augmentation including rotation, zoom, brightness adjustment, and flipping.
  • Compared ResNet50 and DenseNet121 with global average pooling, dropout of 0.5, and a softmax head. Training used Adam with two warm-up epochs and up to 50 fine-tuning epochs.

05

Results

DenseNet121 outperformed ResNet50 within the study’s experimental setup. For comparison, ResNet50 achieved 0.91 accuracy and 0.95 macro AUC in the three-class task, and 0.88 accuracy and 0.91 macro AUC in the five-class task.

06

Limitations & next steps

  • Five-class sensitivity remained limited at 0.68, particularly where severity boundaries were subtle.
  • Patient clinical information was not incorporated; cross-validation and Grad-CAM were not included.

Explore multimodal approaches combining fundus images with clinical information such as patient age and diabetes duration.
