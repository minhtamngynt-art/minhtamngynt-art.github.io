AMD Diagnosis from OCT Images

Performance Evaluation and Explainability Assessment of Deep Learning Architectures for AMD Diagnosis in OCT Images

AICI 2026

Research development

Extending evaluation to stability and explanation behavior, beyond predictive scores alone.

01Overview02My role03Data04Approach05Results06Limitations & next steps

01

Overview

This study compares architectures for classifying OCT images into CNV, Drusen, and Normal. It examines predictive performance, stability across folds, and the image regions emphasized by visual explanations.

02

My role

  • Evaluated ResNet50, ResNet50 with CBAM, and Swin Transformer-Tiny under a shared experimental setup.
  • Applied three-fold cross-validation to assess performance variability.
  • Investigated how CBAM affected Grad-CAM localization and background distraction.

03

Data

  • A balanced subset of 3,000 images from the Mendeley OCT Dataset, with 1,000 images per class.
  • Images were resized to 224 × 224 pixels, converted to RGB, and normalized using ImageNet statistics.
  • No augmentation was used in the baseline experiments.

04

Approach

  • Used two-stage transfer learning: five epochs with the backbone frozen, followed by 15 fine-tuning epochs.
  • Compared a conventional CNN, channel and spatial attention through CBAM, and a vision transformer.
  • Examined model behavior using Grad-CAM and attention maps.

05

Results

ResNet50 achieved the strongest overall predictive performance. The CBAM model achieved the highest reported CNV recall and lower accuracy variability across folds. More localized visualizations were examined as qualitative evidence, not proof of clinical reliability.

06

Limitations & next steps

  • The dataset was relatively small at 3,000 images.
  • Grad-CAM remains a post-hoc analysis tool. More localized heatmaps do not, by themselves, establish faithful explanations or clinical reliability.

Explore intrinsically interpretable, prototype-based architectures whose visual evidence is more closely connected to prediction.
