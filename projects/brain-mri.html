Brain Tumor MRI: Beyond Accuracy

Beyond Accuracy: Comparison of ResNet50 and GWN-Enhanced Models for Brain Tumor MRI Classification with LIME

Asking what supports a prediction and whether the evaluation can be trusted.

01

Overview

Does very high classification accuracy reflect reliable learning? This project compares models for No Tumor, Glioma, and Pituitary Tumor classification while examining background reliance and the risks associated with slice-level dataset splitting.

02

My role

  • Compared standard ResNet50 with a Ghost Weight Normalization (GWN) variant.
  • Evaluated convergence speed and training stability.
  • Applied LIME to inspect image regions influencing predictions and examined how missing patient identifiers limit evaluation confidence.

03

Data

  • A balanced subset of 3,300 images from a public Kaggle Brain Tumor MRI Dataset, with 1,100 images per class.
  • Training / validation / test: 80/10/10, corresponding to 2,640 / 330 / 330 images.
  • Images were resized to 224 × 224 pixels, converted to RGB, and normalized using ImageNet statistics. Augmentation included horizontal flipping and rotation up to 15°.

04

Approach

  • Used five epochs of frozen-backbone training followed by up to 30 fine-tuning epochs.
  • Compared convergence and loss behavior between the baseline and GWN-enhanced model.
  • Used Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations (LIME) to investigate potential shortcut learning.

05

Results

LIME revealed background reliance in both models, raising concerns about shortcut learning and evaluation validity. These scores do not establish generalization to unseen patients. LIME alone does not prove data leakage; it identifies behavior requiring further investigation.

06

Limitations & next steps

  • Patient-wise separation could not be established because patient identifiers were unavailable.
  • The models were not evaluated on independent MRI data from Vietnamese hospitals.

Prioritize patient-level splitting, standardized Vietnamese hospital datasets, and out-of-distribution evaluation.
